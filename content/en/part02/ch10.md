# Chapter 10. Use AI for Learning Without Abandoning Academic Integrity

A generative AI tool produces a working program, but you cannot explain its logic and are unsure whether the assignment permits AI assistance.

## The work a tool performs is not necessarily the learning it produces

Generative AI can reduce the effort of formatting, finding candidate approaches or preparing test data. It can also conceal the fact that the student has not yet learned to select a method or validate a conclusion. Whether its use is appropriate depends on the task's learning objective and published rules. Students remain responsible for citations, personal data, code correctness and research integrity. Teams should specify what tool assistance is allowed, how it is disclosed and who checks the output. Convenience is valuable when it creates room for deeper thought, not when it erases the reasoning that education is meant to develop.

An output is not evidence of personal understanding. Follow the course's current rules on AI, collaboration, citations and independent work; verify generated claims, code, sources and conclusions yourself.

With generative AI, producing an answer and completing the intended learning are different events. Some tasks reasonably permit assistance with formatting, test-data generation or routine code scaffolding. Others require a student to perform the reasoning that the course is designed to develop: selecting a method, explaining assumptions, constructing counterexamples and deciding whether the result is correct. The relevant boundary comes from the assignment's actual learning objectives and its published AI-use rules. Permission to use tools is not permission to conceal their use or submit unverified claims.

Suppose a model generates a sorting algorithm that works on several examples. Correct sample output is not proof of correctness. You still need to test empty inputs, duplicate keys, scale limits and relevant invariants, and explain the algorithm without repeating the model's wording. Bibliographic references, experimental results and images require their own source checks. Never paste classmates' personal data, confidential institutional material or restricted research data into an external service without authorization. The strongest use of AI frees time for demanding intellectual work while leaving the student able to explain and take responsibility for what is submitted.

Where AI is allowed, use it to explore options or debug, then independently test and explain the outcome. Where prohibited or unclear, comply or request clarification before submission.

For this problem, begin with a concrete step: read the assessment policy. Then document permitted tool use. Next test edge cases and citations. Finally explain what you personally verified and what remains uncertain.

Never upload restricted research data, private student information or confidential materials to an unauthorized tool. Invented citations are not acceptable evidence.

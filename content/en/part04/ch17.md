# Chapter 17. Living with Different Personalities and Backgrounds

## A situation you may recognize

Group members use different communication styles, and one student is repeatedly dismissed as difficult or unsuited to teamwork.

## Understand the situation

Differences in language, culture, resources, disability or personality need not become moral labels. Distinguish the practical requirement from stereotypes and informal expectations.

## When conditions differ

If information is lost in spoken meetings, try written summaries or accessible communication. If the problem is discriminatory exclusion, use the institution's protection and complaint processes.

## Actions you can take

1. Name the task affected.
2. ask participants about workable communication options.
3. document agreed arrangements.
4. evaluate whether everyone can participate fairly..

## Boundaries and safety

Respect does not require tolerating harassment or prejudice. Avoid publicly speculating about a person's background, health or ability.

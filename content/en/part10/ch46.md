# Chapter 46. Rejection and Setbacks: Outcomes Are Not the Whole Evidence

## A situation you may recognize

You are not selected for a project or scholarship and assume the decision proves you lack ability or social skills.

## Understand the situation

Results can reflect preparation, limited opportunities, eligibility, other people's decisions and chance. A useful review distinguishes what can be improved from what cannot be inferred.

## When conditions differ

Specific feedback about missing skills suggests targeted practice. A rejection without explanation does not justify inventing a hidden motive.

## Actions you can take

1. Collect the criteria and any available feedback.
2. identify one practical improvement.
3. compare alternative opportunities.
4. choose when to try again or change direction..

## Boundaries and safety

Do not convert one result into a permanent label. You may take a break and seek support without declaring defeat.

> SSCM reading note: Consider what you can demonstrate (C), what support is reachable (N), who has the relevant responsibility (R), what useful contribution can actually be made (V), and whether you have an informed, revisable choice (A). These are questions for this situation, not a score.

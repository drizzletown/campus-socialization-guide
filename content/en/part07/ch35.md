# Chapter 35. Cybersecurity and Online Harassment

Someone impersonates a campus official, asks for login credentials, then threatens to publish private messages.

## Respond to the level of risk, not the tone of a message

Online problems range from minor misunderstandings to account compromise, blackmail and threats of physical harm. Strong authentication, cautious link handling and respect for others' information reduce risk but do not transfer responsibility for abuse to the victim. Document relevant incidents lawfully, avoid retaliation and use platform or institutional reporting channels as appropriate. Imminent danger requires urgent local help. A student experiencing harassment is not deficient at socialization; the environment and its responsible institutions must provide meaningful protection and recovery routes.

Account protection and harassment response require different procedures. Technical defenses reduce exposure; coercion or threats may require institutional, platform or law-enforcement intervention.

Online risk includes account compromise, impersonation, scams, non-consensual disclosure of private information, coordinated harassment and threats. Match the first response to the risk. For a compromised account, use a trusted device to change credentials, inspect active sessions and enable multi-factor authentication. For financial fraud, contact the payment provider and relevant official platform promptly. Where an online threat implies immediate physical danger, prioritize safety in the real world. Do not delete every record out of embarrassment, and do not retaliate by exposing the other person's personal details.

Cyberbullying can be entangled with offline authority and peer pressure. Students who fear retaliation for a report may need protected institutional routes or qualified external support. Preserve relevant context and timestamps lawfully without redistributing harmful material unnecessarily. Bystanders can refuse to amplify abuse, help the affected person reach appropriate support and report clear platform violations. Online independence includes being able to restrict contact, protect information and seek fair remedies; it is not measured by the ability to win a public argument with an aggressor.

A suspicious link calls for independent verification and credential protection. Stalking, blackmail or immediate threats require safety planning and formal help.

For this problem, begin with a concrete step: stop risky transfers or logins. Then use known official channels. Next preserve necessary evidence safely. Finally report to the relevant platform, institution or emergency service.

Never send verification codes or pay a blackmailer simply because they threaten disclosure. Avoid escalating public disputes that spread the harm.

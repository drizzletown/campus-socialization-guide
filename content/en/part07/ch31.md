# Chapter 31. Group Chats and Instant Messaging Without Constant Overload

Important instructions are buried beneath hundreds of chat messages, and members are blamed for missing a deadline.

## Constant connection is not the same as reliable communication

A large group chat can bury important announcements beneath ordinary conversation. Online status can also be misread as a duty to answer immediately. Good coordination separates official notices, open discussion and private matters. Decisions should be recorded in a stable, accessible place with dates, responsibilities and revision history. Members need reasonable response expectations, including those with different schedules or access needs. Muting irrelevant notifications can help individual concentration, but group organizers must also improve the information system rather than place all responsibility on the fastest responder.

Digital communication creates fast connections, but reliable coordination still needs source authority, accessible records and realistic response expectations. A group chat is not a substitute for official notice.

Group chats are convenient but can create hidden coordination costs. A class may have separate channels for courses, general announcements, projects and clubs; urgent decisions can disappear beneath repeated forwards and casual conversation. Separate announcements, discussion, decisions and personal requests. An announcement needs a clear task, deadline and authoritative source. Discussion benefits from an identifiable topic and summary. Silence in a chat should not automatically count as consent to a decision that requires approval. Sensitive personal information should not be circulated in a large group merely because it is easy to post.

Read receipts do not create an obligation to respond continuously. Students have classes, work and legitimate time offline. Agree on realistic response expectations and designate separate urgent contact routes for safety-critical matters. A shared task board or decision log often serves a project better than an endless stream of messages. New and absent members should be able to retrieve the current agreement without asking someone to repeat the entire chat history. Good digital communication reduces uncertainty and treats other people's attention as a limited resource.

If a message is an informal suggestion, verify before acting. If it is an official requirement, confirm it against the designated source and record the deadline.

For this problem, begin with a concrete step: separate official announcements from conversation. Then maintain a private task list. Next ask who is accountable for changes. Finally mute nonurgent messages without ignoring agreed responsibilities.

Never treat a forwarded payment request or personal-data request as legitimate solely because many people shared it.

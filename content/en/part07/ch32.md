# Chapter 32. Social Media and Your Digital Identity

Scrolling through polished student profiles makes your own experiences seem inadequate, and you feel pressured to post more.

## A public profile is a selected view of a life

Social platforms display achievements and experiences stripped of much of their context. Comparing your ordinary days with others' carefully selected highlights is not a fair assessment of progress. Public presentation also has lasting consequences: posts, photographs and screenshots can be copied beyond their intended audience. Before sharing, consider consent, accuracy, visibility and whether a professional portfolio could make the same point with less exposure. A student may participate meaningfully in campus life without maintaining a highly visible online identity.

Online profiles are selected fragments of life. Public identity can help share actual work, but constant self-display is neither required nor evidence of belonging. Consent and lasting visibility matter.

A social-media profile is a selective presentation, not a complete life. Students may see endless posts about awards, internships, travel and impressive friendships, then compare their ordinary Tuesday with somebody else's carefully selected highlights. A more useful question is what you actually want to learn or change, what evidence you have and what support is available. Other people's experiences can offer information without becoming compulsory benchmarks for your life.

Posting also affects people beyond yourself. A group photograph, screenshot, attendance list or location tag may disclose information about classmates who did not agree to publication. Consider consent, audience, long-term visibility and the possibility that content will be copied beyond your intended circle. Privacy settings do not guarantee permanent control. If constant comparison is affecting your concentration or mood, review the accounts you follow and the purpose of your screen time. A coherent online identity can be selective and authentic without requiring continuous self-promotion or the disclosure of every personal experience.

A useful portfolio post can describe a verified project. An emotional post exposing someone else's identity may cause harm or violate rules.

For this problem, begin with a concrete step: ask who will see the post. Then verify facts and permissions. Next remove sensitive data. Finally choose a level of visibility that matches your needs.

Do not publish others' grades, messages, images or laboratory information without permission. Likes are not a reliable measure of human worth.

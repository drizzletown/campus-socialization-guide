# Chapter 41. When Something Seems Unfair: Identify the Decision and the Evidence

## A situation you may recognize

You believe a scholarship or grade was determined unfairly, but cannot tell whether the problem is an error, a difference in eligibility or discrimination.

## Understand the situation

Fairness concerns require procedures and evidence, not merely more effort. Compare the current rule, how it was applied, the affected decision and the role of any reviewing body.

## When conditions differ

A calculation error calls for factual correction. Discriminatory treatment or retaliation may require confidential, independent channels and appropriate protection.

## Actions you can take

1. Locate the applicable policy and deadline.
2. preserve your submission and response.
3. frame a specific question for review.
4. escalate only through lawful and safe channels..

## Boundaries and safety

Do not publicly expose other students' private records or accuse individuals without evidence. Seek support where a power imbalance makes direct contact unsafe.

> SSCM reading note: Consider what you can demonstrate (C), what support is reachable (N), who has the relevant responsibility (R), what useful contribution can actually be made (V), and whether you have an informed, revisable choice (A). These are questions for this situation, not a score.

# Chapter 45. An Immediate Crisis: Protect Life Before Reflecting on Growth

An accident, serious threat or medical emergency occurs during a campus event.

## Emergencies reorder ordinary priorities

Fire, serious injury, imminent violence and acute medical threats demand immediate protection, not an extended reflection exercise. Move to safety where possible, contact local emergency or qualified responders, and provide clear information about the location and ongoing danger. Do not return to a hazardous scene to collect better evidence or share identifying images of affected people. Recovery may later involve healthcare, accommodation, academic arrangements and confidential support. The quality of someone's response to a crisis must not become a personality ranking. Knowing when urgent professional help takes precedence is an essential boundary of everyday social decision-making.

In an emergency the priority is reducing harm and reaching people with the right training and authority. Students should not be required to provide treatment, rescue or investigation beyond their competence.

An emergency changes the order of decisions. Ordinary disagreements may permit careful comparison of explanations; danger calls for immediate protection before detailed analysis. In a fire, serious injury, suspected acute poisoning or credible violent threat, contact local emergency services and responsible on-site personnel, follow appropriate safety instructions and move away from danger where possible. Do not stay in an unsafe area to collect photographs, and do not assume that you must possess rescue qualifications before asking for urgent help.

After the immediate threat, recovery involves different forms of support. Emergency professionals address urgent medical or safety needs; institutions may arrange academic or housing accommodations; trusted people can offer practical companionship. These roles are complementary, not interchangeable. If asked to report an incident, distinguish what you directly witnessed from later hearsay. Protect the privacy of affected people rather than posting identifiable images. Recovery may involve treatment, rest, academic adjustments and continued support. There is no obligation to turn a distressing event into an inspirational lesson immediately. Knowing when to bring in trained professionals is a central part of responsible action.

A routine disagreement can wait for a conversation. Serious injury, imminent danger or a potentially life-threatening situation requires urgent professional intervention.

For this problem, begin with a concrete step: make sure you are not entering danger. Then contact the locally appropriate emergency or campus response service. Next give the location and observed facts. Finally follow qualified instructions.

Do not move a seriously injured person unless the scene itself is unsafe or responders direct you. Avoid spreading images or identifying information.

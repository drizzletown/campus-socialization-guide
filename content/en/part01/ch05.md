# Chapter 05. Make a Bounded Decision When Things Are Difficult

## A situation you may recognize

An invitation promises valuable experience, but its duties are vague and your existing commitments are already substantial.

## Understand the situation

Good decisions begin with a specific event, not a verdict about your character. Test facts, compare possible causes, identify changeable conditions, choose a bounded step, and revise it after observing a response.

## When conditions differ

If the task is unclear, ask for a scope and deadline. If its demands are clear but unaffordable, negotiate or decline instead of accepting an impossible promise.

## Actions you can take

1. State your goal.
2. write verified facts separately from guesses.
3. identify who can change each obstacle.
4. choose a small, reversible action.
5. decide when to reassess..

## Boundaries and safety

Safety, privacy, power imbalances and formal obligations may require institutional support. Personal courage cannot correct every structural problem.

> SSCM reading note: Consider what you can demonstrate (C), what support is reachable (N), who has the relevant responsibility (R), what useful contribution can actually be made (V), and whether you have an informed, revisable choice (A). These are questions for this situation, not a score.

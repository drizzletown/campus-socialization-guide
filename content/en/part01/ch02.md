# Chapter 02. Map the People and Services That Can Support You

You need help with a course, a financial matter, and a difficult roommate situation, but are unsure whom to approach.

## A network is a set of pathways, not a contact count

Think of a support network as a map of functions. A lecturer can explain an assignment but may not decide a scholarship appeal; a friend can accompany you without possessing administrative authority. The map should show official channels, people who can offer limited help, response expectations and alternatives when a route fails. One trusted connection can be valuable for emotional support, while an accessible public procedure may be better for a formal decision. Relationships also have their own limits. Knowing someone never grants an unrestricted claim on their time, authority or private information.

A network map is a guide to reachable support, not a competition to collect contacts. Friends, teachers, student services, and official procedures have different capacities and responsibilities.

A contact list is not a map of usable support. A lecturer may explain an assignment but have no authority over a scholarship appeal. A trusted friend may accompany you to a meeting without knowing the relevant university procedure. For one concrete difficulty, trace three different routes: where accurate information can be found, who can help coordinate the task, and which office has formal authority or a duty to protect students. Record practical constraints such as office hours, reply expectations and alternative routes.

A particularly fragile arrangement relies on one person for everything. If all your internship information comes through a senior student, losing that connection can stop progress. A public careers service, official vacancy notices and employer documentation may provide more dependable access than adding further acquaintances. Emotional support presents a different situation: one mutually trusted relationship can matter more than many weak contacts. Your map should therefore record functions, consent and limits, not popularity. It should also include what you can responsibly offer to others; reciprocity does not require immediate repayment, and access to public services must never depend on personal favors.

A friend may listen without having authority to change a grade. A department office may explain an appeal procedure without needing a personal relationship with you.

For this problem, begin with a concrete step: name the issue precisely. Then find an official first point of contact. Next identify another reliable source if that channel fails. Finally check who can actually decide or provide the needed service.

Do not assume that knowing someone grants access to their time, information, or institutional authority. Protect private contact details.

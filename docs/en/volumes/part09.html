Part 9: When a problem requires protection and institutional authority

Some disagreements can be settled through ordinary discussion. Others involve unequal power, unfair procedures, coercion or immediate danger. Demanding that a student negotiate privately in every case overlooks the responsibilities of institutions and the need for safety.

This part distinguishes a disputed decision, an instance of harassment, a financial disagreement, a formal appeal and an emergency. Each requires different evidence, authority and response priorities. The ability to seek qualified protection is a legitimate form of social action, not proof that someone has failed to manage relationships.

Chapter 34. Use AI as an Assistant, Not as Your Final Judge

You ask an AI system whether to report a conflict or accept an internship and receive a confident recommendation based on incomplete details.

AI assistance still requires human accountability

An AI model can summarize material and propose options without reliably knowing which rules, data or obligations apply in your exact situation. Its fluency should be treated as a candidate explanation, not proof. Before using it with medical information, private messages or unpublished research, check confidentiality and permission. In shared projects, clarify what assistance is allowed and who verifies the final output. A useful system supports comparison, understanding and testing; it should not quietly take over decisions whose consequences the student or organization remains responsible for.

AI can organize questions, compare options and improve clarity, but it does not know all relevant people, rules or actual resources. Its answers may be wrong or unsupported.

AI systems can produce fluent plans, explanations and recommendations, but fluency does not establish truth or compatibility with a university's rules. For a presentation, a model may suggest an outline, raise possible objections or identify terms that need checking. You must still return to course materials, original research and primary data to decide what is justified. Professional problems often turn on assumptions that a polished response omits. A reliable workflow is to generate a candidate, challenge it, verify it and revise it—not to treat generated text as a decision already made.

AI changes teamwork as well as individual learning. If every member submits similar generated paragraphs that nobody can explain, the project may look complete while the actual shared understanding remains weak. Agree on permissible tools, disclosure requirements, who verifies claims and what information cannot be uploaded. Job, health, legal and emergency questions need additional caution: automated suggestions are not authoritative decisions by qualified people. Where data access, organizational approval or another person's consent matters, the responsible human parties must remain able to understand and authorize the action.

For low-stakes drafting, AI may help you prepare a respectful message. For health, contracts, discipline or safety, confirm information with appropriate professionals and official sources.

For this problem, begin with a concrete step: state the goal without exposing sensitive facts. Then ask the system to list assumptions. Next independently verify important claims. Finally make and own the final decision.

Do not disclose someone else's secrets to a chatbot. Avoid labeling people with psychological diagnoses based on AI text.

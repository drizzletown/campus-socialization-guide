Chapter 42. Harassment, Coercion and Control: Put Safety First

A situation you may recognize

Someone with influence over your grades, housing or social life pressures you to comply and threatens consequences if you refuse.

Understand the situation

Harassment and coercion are not ordinary failures of social skill. Assess immediate danger, dependence on the person, available safe alternatives and the role of institutional power.

When conditions differ

A respectful disagreement can be handled through communication. Stalking, threats, blackmail or forced contact call for protective services and documented reporting.

Actions you can take

  1. Move to safety when necessary.
  2. avoid unnecessary contact with the person.
  3. preserve essential evidence without taking new risks.
  4. seek authorized campus or external support..

Boundaries and safety

You need not confront a dangerous person or gather perfect evidence before requesting help. In immediate danger, contact local emergency services.

SSCM reading note: Consider what you can demonstrate (C), what support is reachable (N), who has the relevant responsibility (R), what useful contribution can actually be made (V), and whether you have an informed, revisable choice (A). These are questions for this situation, not a score.

Chapter 36. Choosing a Major or Changing Direction

A situation you may recognize

A difficult course makes you doubt your whole degree program, while friends urge you toward whatever major appears most profitable.

Understand the situation

Distinguish one temporary academic difficulty from enduring interest, the nature of professional work and the actual requirements of another program. Transfer eligibility is governed by current institutional rules.

When conditions differ

If one foundational subject is difficult, address the learning need first. If your broader goals have changed, compare study paths with evidence and realistic costs.

Actions you can take

  1. Read the relevant curricula.
  2. try a lawful introductory project or event.
  3. check transfer deadlines and capacity.
  4. compare staying, electives and alternative pathways..

Boundaries and safety

Do not treat salary rumors or last year's admission requirements as decisive. Major choice is not a permanent personality test.

SSCM reading note: Consider what you can demonstrate (C), what support is reachable (N), who has the relevant responsibility (R), what useful contribution can actually be made (V), and whether you have an informed, revisable choice (A). These are questions for this situation, not a score.

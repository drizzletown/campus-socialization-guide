Try a situation before reading its discussion

Twenty-one hypothetical student questions. Think about the facts, possible explanations, the first practical step and any safety boundary; then compare your reasoning with a suggested response. There is no score.

Academic life

L01 · I want to ask a professor but do not know what to say

Situation: You understand examples in class but cannot solve a modified question alone and hesitate to attend office hours.

Before opening the answer, consider what is known, what is assumed, who has authority, and what you could safely do.

Show a possible response

Possible explanation: Recognition is not the same as independent mastery. A helpful learning record shows what you tried, which assumption failed, and where you need feedback. Asking well-formed questions is part of learning.

Action:

  1. Attempt one unfamiliar task unaided.
  2. identify the exact sticking point.
  3. bring your attempt to a published help session.
  4. test your understanding on a new example afterwards..

Boundary: Respect instructors' response times. If prolonged stress interferes with sleep or daily life, seek appropriate support alongside study advice.

L02 · My submitted assignment has a zero grade

Situation: You want feedback on a grade but fear that questioning the mark will appear disrespectful.

Before opening the answer, consider what is known, what is assumed, who has authority, and what you could safely do.

Show a possible response

Possible explanation: Teaching relationships involve both shared learning objectives and differences in evaluation authority. Effective contact is specific, evidence-based, and made through the appropriate channel.

Action:

  1. Read the syllabus.
  2. prepare the specific question and evidence.
  3. contact the appropriate instructor or office politely.
  4. record the clarification and next action..

Boundary: Do not seek special marks through private connections. Distinguish critical feedback on work from personal humiliation or threats.

L03 · A team member never received repository access

Situation: Only two members finish a group project while others neither reply nor deliver, and the deadline is close.

Before opening the answer, consider what is known, what is assumed, who has authority, and what you could safely do.

Show a possible response

Possible explanation: Group failure may arise from unclear tasks, mismatched skills, inaccessible tools, undefined decision rights or unwillingness to contribute. Evidence about tasks matters more than calling someone lazy.

Action:

  1. Agree on deliverables, owners and dates.
  2. use a shared progress log.
  3. check blockers without public accusations.
  4. adjust assignments or ask the instructor for guidance..

Boundary: Do not silently complete everything while erasing the actual contributions of others. Do not circulate private accusations or fabricate work records.

Shared housing

D01 · My roommate keeps me awake on late-night calls

Situation: One roommate plays videos late at night; another needs sleep. The first discussion turns into accusations in the group chat.

Before opening the answer, consider what is known, what is assumed, who has authority, and what you could safely do.

Show a possible response

Possible explanation: A shared room creates ongoing obligations, but it does not require identical habits. Separate institutional safety rules, collectively agreed arrangements, and individual preferences. Focus on observable effects and workable changes.

Action:

  1. Record the time and impact of the disturbance.
  2. propose a specific quiet-hours arrangement.
  3. agree on a review date.
  4. use residential support channels if the issue persists..

Boundary: Do not retaliate by recording or publishing a roommate's private life. If someone is in immediate danger, prioritize safety over negotiation.

D03 · Someone takes my belongings and excludes me

Situation: A disagreement becomes personal, with angry messages and competing accounts of what happened.

Before opening the answer, consider what is known, what is assumed, who has authority, and what you could safely do.

Show a possible response

Possible explanation: A conflict includes facts, impact, expectations and possible changes to the relationship. Repair takes meaningful participation and changed behavior; one person cannot guarantee reconciliation.

Action:

  1. Write down specific events.
  2. explain the impact without diagnosing motives.
  3. propose a clear boundary or remedy.
  4. decide whether renewed contact is safe and useful..

Boundary: Do not circulate screenshots for public humiliation. Reconciliation is optional when safety or respect is at stake.

Research

R01 · How can I approach a research supervisor?

Situation: You are interested in a laboratory but do not know whether to contact a professor, prepare skills first or wait for a formal call.

Before opening the answer, consider what is known, what is assumed, who has authority, and what you could safely do.

Show a possible response

Possible explanation: Research access depends on task fit, entry procedures, mentoring capacity and safety—not merely confidence or personal acquaintances. Starting with a manageable question often yields better learning than promising too much.

Action:

  1. Review the group's public work.
  2. identify your relevant coursework and gaps.
  3. ask about tasks, supervision and expected time.
  4. agree on a starter activity and feedback point..

Boundary: Do not enter a laboratory, access restricted data or operate equipment without the required permission and training.

R02 · My supervisor wants lab data on a personal drive

Situation: A deadline approaches, and team members propose skipping an approval, hiding anomalous data or deciding author order informally.

Before opening the answer, consider what is known, what is assumed, who has authority, and what you could safely do.

Show a possible response

Possible explanation: Responsible research requires valid permissions, honest records, safe procedures, reproducibility and fair attribution. A student's role in a lab can bring real obligations that require support and training.

Action:

  1. Confirm approvals and training before work.
  2. maintain data provenance and version history.
  3. discuss contributions early.
  4. report deviations and incidents through authorized channels..

Boundary: Never alter data to fit a hypothesis. Do not negotiate silence about research misconduct in exchange for authorship or credit.

Student organizations

C01 · The club asks me to pay activity costs up front

Situation: Members are asked to fund an activity without seeing a budget or knowing who approved it.

Before opening the answer, consider what is known, what is assumed, who has authority, and what you could safely do.

Show a possible response

Possible explanation: Shared resources require transparent procedures, legitimate decision rights and usable records. Popular enthusiasm does not replace consent, accounting or risk management.

Action:

  1. Request the governing rules.
  2. list costs and approvals.
  3. record a fair decision process.
  4. make spending and reconciliation available to authorized members..

Boundary: Do not publicly accuse others of theft without evidence. Do not pay into unverified personal accounts for institutional expenses.

C02 · Only friends of the officers receive recognition

Situation: You believe a scholarship or grade was determined unfairly, but cannot tell whether the problem is an error, a difference in eligibility or discrimination.

Before opening the answer, consider what is known, what is assumed, who has authority, and what you could safely do.

Show a possible response

Possible explanation: Fairness concerns require procedures and evidence, not merely more effort. Compare the current rule, how it was applied, the affected decision and the role of any reviewing body.

Action:

C03 · A team outing is treated as compulsory

Situation: A project that began as occasional volunteering now demands daily attendance and criticizes anyone who withdraws.

Before opening the answer, consider what is known, what is assumed, who has authority, and what you could safely do.

Show a possible response

Possible explanation: Participation is sustainable only when duties, learning value, resources and freedom to change are reasonably aligned. Leaving can be a responsible response when circumstances change.

Action:

  1. Review the original commitment.
  2. identify remaining deliverables.
  3. propose a realistic handover.
  4. document agreements and protect confidential information..

Boundary: Do not abandon urgent safety obligations without arranging proper transfer. Others cannot demand unlimited service as proof of loyalty.

Relationships

P01 · My partner demands my phone password

Situation: A friend asks you to impersonate them in attendance records; another insists you share private photos to prove loyalty.

Before opening the answer, consider what is known, what is assumed, who has authority, and what you could safely do.

Show a possible response

Possible explanation: Boundaries identify which acts are authorized. Supporting someone never obliges you to lie, breach policies, share personal information or be available at all hours. Rejection can coexist with care.

Action:

  1. Describe the request accurately.
  2. separate your obligations from voluntary help.
  3. refuse clearly if necessary.
  4. suggest an acceptable route and document serious pressure..

Boundary: Respect others' refusals as well. If declining creates a credible risk of retaliation, prioritize a safe channel and support person.

P03 · My former partner will not stop contacting me

Situation: A partner expects instant replies and constant location sharing, describing hesitation as proof of insufficient love.

Before opening the answer, consider what is known, what is assumed, who has authority, and what you could safely do.

Show a possible response

Possible explanation: Care and intimacy involve trust, explicit consent, shared expectations and individual responsibilities. Relationships do not create automatic access to devices, whereabouts or private messages.

Action:

  1. State your needs and availability.
  2. ask what both people freely agree to.
  3. set revisitable limits on time and information sharing.
  4. seek support if pressure escalates..

Boundary: Consent can change and be withdrawn. Do not use threats of self-harm or exposure of private material to prevent a breakup.

Jobs and internships

J01 · I receive no answers to job applications

Situation: You send applications without responses and begin to believe you have no worthwhile skills.

Before opening the answer, consider what is known, what is assumed, who has authority, and what you could safely do.

Show a possible response

Possible explanation: Recruitment outcomes depend on opportunity supply, timing, qualification filters and how evidence matches role requirements. A rejection does not identify its cause by itself.

Action:

  1. Read the job's actual duties.
  2. map them to verifiable projects.
  3. describe your role and outcomes honestly.
  4. check employer legitimacy and contract conditions..

Boundary: Do not submit confidential employer materials or invented experience. Never pay an unverified recruiter merely for the promise of a job.

Show a possible response

Q03 · Someone collapses during a campus activity

Situation: An accident, serious threat or medical emergency occurs during a campus event.

Before opening the answer, consider what is known, what is assumed, who has authority, and what you could safely do.

Show a possible response

Possible explanation: In an emergency the priority is reducing harm and reaching people with the right training and authority. Students should not be required to provide treatment, rescue or investigation beyond their competence.

Action:

  1. Make sure you are not entering danger.
  2. contact the locally appropriate emergency or campus response service.
  3. give the location and observed facts.
  4. follow qualified instructions..

Boundary: Do not move a seriously injured person unless the scene itself is unsafe or responders direct you. Avoid spreading images or identifying information.

Academic life

L01 · I want to ask a professor but do not know what to say

Which facts are missing, and who can safely act?

Compare a possible response

Context: You understand examples in class but cannot solve a modified question alone and hesitate to attend office hours.

Explanation: Following a demonstrated solution is easier than selecting the method independently. A student who can repeat a calculus derivation may still be unable to decide which theorem applies to a changed problem. To learn, practise identifying the problem, testing conditions and explaining the failure of an attempted method. A well-prepared request for help includes what you know, what you tried and where you became uncertain. Yet not every difficulty is cognitive: workload, poor sleep or inaccessible feedback may also be decisive. Educational support should make the relevant requirements and channels visible, rather than treating all obstacles as insufficient diligence.

How conditions matter: If you cannot explain the underlying concept, revisit fundamentals. If you understand it but misread the assignment, clarify requirements rather than endlessly practicing unrelated problems.

Practical step: For this problem, begin with a concrete step: attempt one unfamiliar task unaided. Then identify the exact sticking point. Next bring your attempt to a published help session. Finally test your understanding on a new example afterwards.

Boundary: Respect instructors' response times. If prolonged stress interferes with sleep or daily life, seek appropriate support alongside study advice.

L02 · My submitted assignment has a zero grade

Which facts are missing, and who can safely act?

Compare a possible response

Context: You want feedback on a grade but fear that questioning the mark will appear disrespectful.

Explanation: The same instructor may explain an idea, assess a task or supervise research. These roles do not confer unlimited authority over the student's life. A grade query requires the published criteria and a specific item; a research enquiry requires a task and a supervision arrangement. Private goodwill cannot automatically replace an official review or admission procedure. Students should communicate clearly and respectfully, while institutions should provide accessible routes and realistic response expectations. A delayed answer is a reason to check timing and procedure, not immediate evidence of personal hostility.

How conditions matter: Teaching relationships involve both shared learning objectives and differences in evaluation authority. Effective contact is specific, evidence-based, and made through the appropriate channel.

Practical step: For this problem, begin with a concrete step: read the syllabus. Then prepare the specific question and evidence. Next contact the appropriate instructor or office politely. Finally record the clarification and next action.

Boundary: Do not seek special marks through private connections. Distinguish critical feedback on work from personal humiliation or threats.

L03 · A team member never received repository access

Which facts are missing, and who can safely act?

Compare a possible response

Context: Only two members finish a group project while others neither reply nor deliver, and the deadline is close.

Explanation: Dividing a project into four jobs does not guarantee a coherent result. An interface depends on an API; testing depends on a working build; documentation depends on decisions that the team actually made. Reliable teamwork identifies dependencies, acceptance criteria and change-notification rules early. Unequal contributions need interpretation: the difficulty may concern access, preparation, a genuine hardship or repeated refusal to fulfil an agreement. The response should vary accordingly. If unresolved, a factual record and the course's formal procedures are more appropriate than humiliation or last-minute silent rescue.

How conditions matter: If a member lacks access or training, fix that barrier. If duties are documented and repeatedly ignored, use the course's formal contribution and escalation rules.

Practical step: For this problem, begin with a concrete step: agree on deliverables, owners and dates. Then use a shared progress log. Next check blockers without public accusations. Finally adjust assignments or ask the instructor for guidance.

Boundary: Do not silently complete everything while erasing the actual contributions of others. Do not circulate private accusations or fabricate work records.

Shared housing

D01 · My roommate keeps me awake on late-night calls

Which facts are missing, and who can safely act?

Compare a possible response

Context: One roommate plays videos late at night; another needs sleep. The first discussion turns into accusations in the group chat.

Explanation: Roommates share space for long periods without necessarily choosing one another. Conflicts about sleep, visitors and storage are therefore matters of access to shared resources, not simply signs of incompatible character. A practical agreement states observable behavior, allows a trial period and respects official safety rules. A majority vote cannot remove a person's basic need for safe rest. Repeated violations may require residence staff or formal accommodation arrangements; threats, theft or invasive recording require safety and institutional support rather than another private negotiation.

How conditions matter: If the new pattern resolves the disturbance, a clear shared rule may have been sufficient. If agreements are repeatedly ignored, housing staff or a formal room-change procedure may need to become involved. A third turning point arises if one roommate threatens property damage or exposure of private information. The issue has moved from conflicting routines to safety. A student facing threats should prioritize protection and appropriate institutional support, not another private negotiation. Communication helps manage ordinary differences; it is not a duty to repeatedly confront the source of a credible threat.

Practical step: For this problem, begin with a concrete step: record the time and impact of the disturbance. Then propose a specific quiet-hours arrangement. Next agree on a review date. Finally use residential support channels if the issue persists.

Boundary: Do not retaliate by recording or publishing a roommate's private life. If someone is in immediate danger, prioritize safety over negotiation.

D02 · My roommate threatens to reveal private information

Which facts are missing, and who can safely act?

Compare a possible response

Context: Someone with influence over your grades, housing or social life pressures you to comply and threatens consequences if you refuse.

Explanation: Ordinary disagreement assumes that both people can express preferences without fear of retaliation. Stalking, threats to disclose intimate information or pressure based on academic authority can remove that freedom. A seemingly voluntary agreement may therefore be unsafe to interpret at face value. Prioritize immediate safety, appropriate confidential support and the relevant protection procedure. Do not insist on a face-to-face reconciliation when a person is afraid. Friends should offer informed accompaniment rather than force public disclosure or undertake vigilante retaliation.

How conditions matter: Harassment and coercion are not ordinary failures of social skill. Assess immediate danger, dependence on the person, available safe alternatives and the role of institutional power.

Practical step: For this problem, begin with a concrete step: move to safety when necessary. Then avoid unnecessary contact with the person. Next preserve essential evidence without taking new risks. Finally seek authorized campus or external support.

Boundary: You need not confront a dangerous person or gather perfect evidence before requesting help. In immediate danger, contact local emergency services.

D03 · Someone takes my belongings and excludes me

Which facts are missing, and who can safely act?

Compare a possible response

Context: A disagreement becomes personal, with angry messages and competing accounts of what happened.

Explanation: After an argument, people often rush to decide whether the other person is good or bad. A missed deadline or broken agreement may first require a factual repair: acknowledgment, correction and a better process. An apology without changed behavior may not restore trust, and the harmed person need not immediately resume closeness. Some relationships can continue professionally without returning to friendship. Where intimidation or power imbalance is involved, a neutral channel or protective service may be safer than a forced meeting intended to create reconciliation.

How conditions matter: A conflict includes facts, impact, expectations and possible changes to the relationship. Repair takes meaningful participation and changed behavior; one person cannot guarantee reconciliation.

Practical step: For this problem, begin with a concrete step: write down specific events. Then explain the impact without diagnosing motives. Next propose a clear boundary or remedy. Finally decide whether renewed contact is safe and useful.

Boundary: Do not circulate screenshots for public humiliation. Reconciliation is optional when safety or respect is at stake.

Research

R01 · How can I approach a research supervisor?

Which facts are missing, and who can safely act?

Compare a possible response

Context: You are interested in a laboratory but do not know whether to contact a professor, prepare skills first or wait for a formal call.

Explanation: Students entering research often ask first about publications, but useful early work can be reproducing a result, checking a reference, documenting an experiment or cleaning authorized data. The task has educational value when its purpose, supervision and evaluation are clear. A research group should explain entry conditions, safety training, access permissions and expectations about acknowledgment or authorship. The student's responsibility grows from following a supervised method to selecting and defending methods independently. Neither enthusiasm nor a supervisor's seniority removes the need for evidence and ethical safeguards.

How conditions matter: If a meeting is offered, confirm the learning goal, available supervision and safety requirements before making an unlimited time commitment. Restricted datasets and hazardous equipment require authorization. Even an apparently simple reproduction task needs clear acceptance criteria. No reply may mean that a lab has no capacity or appropriate project; it is not a reliable verdict on the student's potential. Universities can reduce unnecessary inequality by publishing entry routes and training opportunities, so that access does not depend entirely on knowing a researcher personally.

Practical step: For this problem, begin with a concrete step: review the group's public work. Then identify your relevant coursework and gaps. Next ask about tasks, supervision and expected time. Finally agree on a starter activity and feedback point.

Boundary: Do not enter a laboratory, access restricted data or operate equipment without the required permission and training.

R02 · My supervisor wants lab data on a personal drive

Which facts are missing, and who can safely act?

Compare a possible response

Context: A deadline approaches, and team members propose skipping an approval, hiding anomalous data or deciding author order informally.

Explanation: Research records allow other people to evaluate how a conclusion was reached. Preserving data provenance, explaining exclusions, recording code versions and checking references are not administrative decoration. Authorship should reflect relevant contribution and applicable institutional or disciplinary standards, not a person's rank alone. Laboratory safety adds a more immediate constraint: students must not operate dangerous equipment or handle restricted information without training and permission. Identifying a hazard and stopping to ask is part of professional competence. An impressive result obtained through unsafe or unverifiable practice is not a dependable scholarly contribution.

How conditions matter: Responsible research requires valid permissions, honest records, safe procedures, reproducibility and fair attribution. A student's role in a lab can bring real obligations that require support and training.

Practical step: For this problem, begin with a concrete step: confirm approvals and training before work. Then maintain data provenance and version history. Next discuss contributions early. Finally report deviations and incidents through authorized channels.

Boundary: Never alter data to fit a hypothesis. Do not negotiate silence about research misconduct in exchange for authorship or credit.

Student organizations

C01 · The club asks me to pay activity costs up front

Which facts are missing, and who can safely act?

Compare a possible response

Context: Members are asked to fund an activity without seeing a budget or knowing who approved it.

Explanation: A class election, shared budget or club event can generate disagreement even among friendly people. Public procedures help identify who proposes, who decides, how money is authorized and how objections are considered. Majority preference does not justify violating basic rights or bypassing university rules. Written records and appropriate approvals protect participants and communal resources. Disagreement is not disloyalty when it concerns relevant evidence, fairness and viable alternatives. An organization earns trust when its decisions can be explained and revised.

How conditions matter: Shared resources require transparent procedures, legitimate decision rights and usable records. Popular enthusiasm does not replace consent, accounting or risk management.

Practical step: For this problem, begin with a concrete step: request the governing rules. Then list costs and approvals. Next record a fair decision process. Finally make spending and reconciliation available to authorized members.

Boundary: Do not publicly accuse others of theft without evidence. Do not pay into unverified personal accounts for institutional expenses.

C02 · Only friends of the officers receive recognition

Which facts are missing, and who can safely act?

Compare a possible response

Context: You believe a scholarship or grade was determined unfairly, but cannot tell whether the problem is an error, a difference in eligibility or discrimination.

Explanation: A disappointing mark, scholarship outcome or placement may involve a substantive disagreement, an administrative error, an inaccessible process or discrimination. These cases require different evidence and authorities. Identify the decision, the criteria and any verifiable discrepancy before requesting a review. Students should not be expected to overcome opaque access rules through private networking. Institutions must make applications, reasons and appropriate appeals sufficiently understandable. A fair process need not guarantee the result a claimant prefers, but it should allow accountable examination of the relevant facts.

How conditions matter: Fairness concerns require procedures and evidence, not merely more effort. Compare the current rule, how it was applied, the affected decision and the role of any reviewing body.

Practical step: For this problem, begin with a concrete step: locate the applicable policy and deadline. Then preserve your submission and response. Next frame a specific question for review. Finally escalate only through lawful and safe channels.

Boundary: Do not publicly expose other students' private records or accuse individuals without evidence. Seek support where a power imbalance makes direct contact unsafe.

C03 · A team outing is treated as compulsory

Which facts are missing, and who can safely act?

Compare a possible response

Context: A project that began as occasional volunteering now demands daily attendance and criticizes anyone who withdraws.

Explanation: Circumstances change after a student accepts a position or project. Responsible departure means identifying current duties, outstanding commitments and items that need transfer. It does not mean remaining indefinitely because others have failed to plan succession. A well-run group should be able to replace members, document processes and agree on reasonable handovers. Where participation has become dangerous or coercive, safety may require immediate withdrawal and formal help. Entry, cooperation and departure all belong to the same social learning process.

How conditions matter: If the burden is temporary and meaningful, negotiate scope. If promised support never appears or demands become coercive, pursue an orderly exit or seek institutional help.

Practical step: For this problem, begin with a concrete step: review the original commitment. Then identify remaining deliverables. Next propose a realistic handover. Finally document agreements and protect confidential information.

Boundary: Do not abandon urgent safety obligations without arranging proper transfer. Others cannot demand unlimited service as proof of loyalty.

Relationships

P01 · My partner demands my phone password

Which facts are missing, and who can safely act?

Compare a possible response

Context: A friend asks you to impersonate them in attendance records; another insists you share private photos to prove loyalty.

Explanation: Saying yes to every request produces unclear commitments and can make relationships more fragile. Students may need to decline lending accounts, completing another person's work, sharing messages or joining an unsafe event. A respectful refusal can acknowledge the request, state what is not possible and offer a limited alternative where appropriate. No one is required to purchase permission to say no by supplying endless explanations. Similarly, friends and partners must have room to change their minds about private information and shared activities. Trust depends partly on knowing that consent is genuine.

How conditions matter: Boundaries identify which acts are authorized. Supporting someone never obliges you to lie, breach policies, share personal information or be available at all hours. Rejection can coexist with care.

Practical step: For this problem, begin with a concrete step: describe the request accurately. Then separate your obligations from voluntary help. Next refuse clearly if necessary. Finally suggest an acceptable route and document serious pressure.

Boundary: Respect others' refusals as well. If declining creates a credible risk of retaliation, prioritize a safe channel and support person.

P03 · My former partner will not stop contacting me

Which facts are missing, and who can safely act?

Compare a possible response

Context: A partner expects instant replies and constant location sharing, describing hesitation as proof of insufficient love.

Explanation: An intimate relationship can enrich a student's life while affecting time, friendships and future plans. Care and control are not the same. Consent concerns specific acts and can be revised; having agreed to one activity does not authorize access to a phone, location or body on future occasions. Discussions about travel, publicity, intimacy or graduation plans require separate attention to each person's wishes and realistic obligations. Threats to reveal private images, stalking or coercion call for safety and qualified support, not negotiations about proving love.

How conditions matter: If both wish to continue, agree on realistic contact, visits and a date for reviewing the arrangement. If one cannot accept the distance, ending the relationship respectfully may be an honest outcome. Coercive threats are different: “give up the offer or I will publish your private information” is not a normal negotiation. It calls for protection and appropriate support. Whether a couple stays together or separates, both people should retain room for education, work, friendships and informed choices. Agreement is meaningful only when refusal remains possible.

Practical step: For this problem, begin with a concrete step: state your needs and availability. Then ask what both people freely agree to. Next set revisitable limits on time and information sharing. Finally seek support if pressure escalates.

Boundary: Consent can change and be withdrawn. Do not use threats of self-harm or exposure of private material to prevent a breakup.

Jobs and internships

J01 · I receive no answers to job applications

Which facts are missing, and who can safely act?

Compare a possible response

Context: You send applications without responses and begin to believe you have no worthwhile skills.

Explanation: Formatting a résumé matters, but employers need evidence of someone who can address a specific set of tasks. Describe a project by its problem, method, validation and your actual responsibility. Investigate the role, working conditions, contract and recruitment authority before sharing sensitive documents. Requests for deposits or unrelated financial information warrant caution. Rejection can reflect an unavailable role, changing requirements or poor fit, rather than a judgment on personal worth. A student should evaluate whether a job deserves acceptance as carefully as an employer evaluates whether a candidate fits.

How conditions matter: Recruitment outcomes depend on opportunity supply, timing, qualification filters and how evidence matches role requirements. A rejection does not identify its cause by itself.

Practical step: For this problem, begin with a concrete step: read the job's actual duties. Then map them to verifiable projects. Next describe your role and outcomes honestly. Finally check employer legitimacy and contract conditions.

Boundary: Do not submit confidential employer materials or invented experience. Never pay an unverified recruiter merely for the promise of a job.

J03 · My internship demands client data by personal email

Which facts are missing, and who can safely act?

Compare a possible response

Context: Your internship supervisor asks you to copy customer data to a personal computer for weekend work.

Explanation: An internship may involve live systems, clients, restricted data or legal obligations. Students need a clear supervisor, permissions, review procedures and a way to report mistakes. Initiative does not justify deploying unfamiliar code or copying company data into a private account. For a future portfolio, describe the problem and your role without disclosing protected information. A placement is also something the student should evaluate: meaningful training, safe conditions, transparent payment and legitimate expectations matter. Enduring exploitation is not a necessary lesson in professional maturity.

How conditions matter: Students entering workplaces face real rules on safety, data, quality, working hours and supervision. A task being useful to a company does not itself establish permission to perform it.

Practical step: For this problem, begin with a concrete step: clarify the task, approved tools and access rights. Then agree on review and delivery standards. Next raise risks early. Finally document what was authorized.

Boundary: Do not export confidential data, share credentials or bypass security reviews to impress an evaluator. Seek school or formal workplace support for coercive requests.

Q03 · Someone collapses during a campus activity

Which facts are missing, and who can safely act?

Compare a possible response

Context: An accident, serious threat or medical emergency occurs during a campus event.

Explanation: Fire, serious injury, imminent violence and acute medical threats demand immediate protection, not an extended reflection exercise. Move to safety where possible, contact local emergency or qualified responders, and provide clear information about the location and ongoing danger. Do not return to a hazardous scene to collect better evidence or share identifying images of affected people. Recovery may later involve healthcare, accommodation, academic arrangements and confidential support. The quality of someone's response to a crisis must not become a personality ranking. Knowing when urgent professional help takes precedence is an essential boundary of everyday social decision-making.

How conditions matter: In an emergency the priority is reducing harm and reaching people with the right training and authority. Students should not be required to provide treatment, rescue or investigation beyond their competence.

Practical step: For this problem, begin with a concrete step: make sure you are not entering danger. Then contact the locally appropriate emergency or campus response service. Next give the location and observed facts. Finally follow qualified instructions.

Boundary: Do not move a seriously injured person unless the scene itself is unsafe or responders direct you. Avoid spreading images or identifying information.

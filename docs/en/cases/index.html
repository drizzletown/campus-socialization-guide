WORKED EXAMPLES

Twelve cases in context

These hypothetical situations illustrate how different facts lead to different choices.

CASE 01

Considering a Research Position

Two capable students have not applied to a lab. One is already committed to a competition; the other does not know the project's requirements.

CASE 02

A Team Project Falls on Two People

A five-person programming team is near its deadline, but only two members have submitted usable work.

CASE 03

Late-Night Videos in a Shared Room

One roommate needs to sleep; another frequently watches videos aloud after lights-out, and the discussion becomes accusatory.

CASE 04

Requesting a Grade Review

A student suspects an exam question was incorrectly marked and wonders whether a friend should ask the instructor for a favor.

CASE 05

A Friend Asks for Fake Attendance

A friend wants you to sign them in for a missed class and says no one will discover it.

CASE 06

Club Membership Fees Without Accounting

A club collects fees but refuses to explain spending; members who ask are accused of disloyalty.

CASE 07

A Dispute over Research Authorship

Two students made different contributions to data analysis and experimental design and disagree about author order.

CASE 08

Pressure to Share Live Location

One partner regards continuous location access as proof of trust while the other wants privacy.

CASE 09

Customer Data on a Personal Laptop

An intern is told to copy customer names and contact details onto a personal computer to work from home.

CASE 10

An Upfront Recruitment Deposit

A recruiter promises employment in exchange for a training deposit and asks for identity and bank details.

CASE 11

False Stories from Anonymous Accounts

An anonymous account spreads a rumor about a student, and friends recommend public retaliation.

CASE 12

Graduation with an Uncertain Direction

A graduating student has good marks but no fixed career plan and feels pressured to commit to a ten-year strategy.
